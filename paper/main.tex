\title{Feasibility Limits of Certified Low-Precision Greedy Decoding:\\
A Preregistered Study with an Oracle Ceiling}
\author{Le Duy Phuc\\Independent researcher}
\date{November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Lossless inference schemes for large language models (LLMs) often hope to compute
most of the work in low precision and to \emph{certify}, with a deterministic bound,
that the greedy token is unchanged. We test this hope with kill-tests whose pass
thresholds were written down before each experiment, on two pretrained models
(Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat) and 1{,}024 held-out decoding
positions each. Three families miss their feasibility thresholds by wide margins.
(i) Coordinate-wise error boxes at the final hidden state: after dropping four
mantissa bits, the coarse argmax already agrees with the reference at 92--97\% of
positions, yet even an \emph{oracle} that knows the true error certifies only
4.7\%/12.1\% (truncation) and 18.6\%/26.5\% (rounding), against a 95\% target. We
show that no sound certificate of this family with the same center can exceed the
oracle, and we verify every oracle decision with rigorous floating-point
enclosures (zero unresolved positions), so the ceiling is established on these
data. (ii) Exact maximum inner product search over the LM head with cluster bounds
reads 107--111\% of the head's logical bytes. (iii) Dimension-adaptive tail-norm
pruning, in a calibrated order or in an SVD basis, still reads 83--100\%. As a side
result, the native BF16 LM head on Apple MPS disagrees with the exact-real argmax
of the same hidden state at 0.88\% of positions. A candidate certificate that is
sound under a stated arithmetic model recovers the exact token at every position;
with FP32 output it needs refinement at 0.68\% (Qwen) and 1.76\% (TinyLlama) of
positions, meeting the 1\% refinement criterion for one model only. Latency was
not evaluated. Code, preregistrations and raw results are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Low-precision inference trades exactness for speed. Several lines of work try to
keep the speed while restoring exactness: lossless weight compression
\cite{zhang2025dfloat11,hershcovitch2025zipnn}, self-speculative decoding with a
quantized draft verified by the full model
\cite{zhao2025qspec,tiwari2025quantspec,zhao2025speq}, and fast search over the
output vocabulary \cite{loretz2026output}. A tempting further step is to skip
verification altogether: compute in low precision, then prove with a cheap
deterministic bound that the greedy decision could not have changed.

This paper reports what happened when we tried. An earlier prototype of ours
certified every intermediate rounding cell of a synthetic Transformer and accepted
zero of 6.5 million trunk rows. We therefore moved the certificate to the only place
where a decision is made, the greedy argmax, and asked three questions, each with a
pass threshold fixed in advance:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can a coordinate-wise error box on the final hidden state certify the greedy
token for most positions? (Section~\ref{sec:oracle-ceiling})
\item Can exact search over the LM head certify the greedy token while reading a
small fraction of its weights? (Section~\ref{sec:results})
\item Can the native low-precision LM head be made provably exact with rare
refinement? (Section~\ref{sec:certified-head})
\end{enumerate}
The answers are no, no, and partially. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item an oracle diagnostic that turns one cheap experiment into a bound on a whole
family of certification methods (Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-ceiling}); the underlying
box optimization is standard, the use as a feasibility test is ours;
\item preregistered negative results on two pretrained models, with prompt-level
bootstrap intervals, an audited exact reference, and rigorous verification of the
oracle decisions;
\item a greedy-head certificate with zero observed errors, and the measurement
that its refinement rate is dominated by BF16 output rounding.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preregistered methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Process.} Each test has a written preregistration with its pass
thresholds, written before the corresponding experiment and released verbatim. The
files were kept privately and not publicly timestamped at the time, so their
precedence rests on our record rather than on an external registry. We distinguish three
kinds of evidence: development runs (256 positions of Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct), the
preregistered held-out replication reported in Table~\ref{tab:main} (new prompts
and a second model), and exploratory diagnostics, which are labeled as such.
Thresholds were never changed after a run. Criteria that were not run because the
preregistered stopping rule ended a line of work are reported as not evaluated.

\paragraph{Models and data.} Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (tied head, $V{=}151{,}936$,
$d{=}896$) and TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0 ($V{=}32{,}000$, $d{=}2{,}048$), original
BF16 weights. Held-out prompts: WikiText-2 test paragraphs \#16--\#79 (at least 40
words, no headings) and GSM8K test questions \#16--\#79 in each model's chat
template, truncated to 48 tokens. The reference model generates 8 greedy tokens per
prompt with end-of-sequence suppressed, giving 1{,}024 positions per model.
Calibration data, used only to order coordinates in v21b, come from WikiText-2
validation and GSM8K train. Hidden states are taken after the final RMSNorm.
Forward passes run on Apple M4 (PyTorch MPS); certificates are evaluated in FP64
on CPU.

\paragraph{Coarse model (v20).} The coarse model uses the same weights except that
every linear projection inside the Transformer blocks (query, key, value, output,
gate, up and down) keeps only the top 3 of the 7 explicit BF16 mantissa bits, by
truncation or by round-to-nearest with ties rounded away from zero in magnitude.
Embeddings, normalization weights and the LM head are unchanged. Reference and
coarse model are both run teacher-forced on the same token sequence (prompt plus
the reference's 8 greedy tokens) in independent forward passes, so the two hidden
states at a position share the same prefix.

\paragraph{Search methods (v21).} \emph{v21a} is a binary tree over the rows of
$W$, split at the median of the highest-variance coordinate down to leaves of at
most 64 rows. Each node stores an FP32 centroid $c$, a ball radius $R$, and radii
$R_g$ for 8 contiguous coordinate blocks; its upper bound on $w_i^\top h$ is
$c^\top h+\min(R\|h\|_2,\sum_g R_g\|h_g\|_2)$. Nodes are visited best-first until
the bound falls below the best exact logit found. \emph{v21b} orders coordinates by
the calibration energy $\mathbb{E}[h_k^2]\sum_i w_{ik}^2$ and accumulates partial
inner products at checkpoints $64,128,256,\dots,d$. At each checkpoint $P$, a row
survives while $s_i^{(P)}+\|w_{i,>P}\|_2\|h_{>P}\|_2\geq L$, where the incumbent $L$
is the exact logit of the current partial leader (its tail is read and counted).
\emph{v21c} applies the same procedure after rotating rows and query into the
eigenbasis of $W^\top W$ (the right singular vectors of $W$), in decreasing
eigenvalue order. It is an SVD-based ablation inspired by FEXIPRO
\cite{li2017fexipro}, not a reproduction of it: integer approximation and the other
FEXIPRO components are omitted, and the rotated coordinates are generously counted at
2 bytes each with the query rotation not counted.

\paragraph{Byte accounting.} $\rho$ is the number of logical bytes read (BF16
weights at 2 bytes per coordinate, 4 bytes per tail norm or row index, $4d+44$ bytes
per tree node) divided by the bytes of one dense pass, $2Vd$. This counts logical
accesses, not measured DRAM traffic; $\rho>1$ means more than a dense pass.

\paragraph{Numerical soundness.} The exact reference is the exact-real argmax of
$Wh$. We compute $Wh$ in FP64 and verify at every position that the FP64 gap between
the top token and every rival exceeds the sum of their error bounds
$\gamma^{(64)}_d\sum_k|w_kh_k|$. This holds at 2{,}048/2{,}048 positions, with the
smallest gap more than $10^7$ times the bound. For v20, every accepted and rejected
position is re-verified with enclosures on the FP64 dot products (differences of
BF16 values are exact in FP64): a position counts as rejected only if some competitor
has a provably nonpositive margin upper bound. For v21a/b, the bounds include a
relative guard of $10^{-9}$, tree radii are inflated by a relative $10^{-6}$, and
tail norms are rounded upward by at least half an FP32 ulp, all several orders of magnitude above the FP64 rounding error of
the quantities they protect. Final tokens of all v21 runs matched the exact
reference. Any further conservatism can only enlarge bounds and keep rows alive
longer, so it cannot lower $\rho$; the fail verdicts are therefore robust to these
details. v21c has no enclosure for the rotation error and is an optimistic
screening experiment, which again can only flatter its $\rho$.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Rates are means over positions with 95\% intervals from a
prompt-level bootstrap (2{,}000 resamples), since the 8 positions of one prompt
are correlated.

Qwen positions agree (v20 3.5\%, v21a 110.9\%, v21b 83.4\%, v22 10.2\%).

\paragraph{v20: the oracle ceiling is far below target.} The oracle knows the true
coordinate errors $r^\star=|h-\hat h|$, yet certifies at most 26.5\% of positions,
although the coarse argmax already equals the reference at 92--97\% of them. The
gap is therefore a property of the box representation, not of the quantizer's
accuracy. The rigorous re-verification classifies all 4{,}096 oracle decisions
(two models, two quantizers) as proven accept or proven reject, with none
unresolved, so the measured rates are the mathematical oracle rates. The median
headroom $s^\star$ is 0.20 (Qwen) and 0.23 (TinyLlama) with truncation and
0.37/0.40 with rounding: the oracle radius would have to shrink by a factor of
2.5--5 at the median position before certification succeeds. In an exploratory
run on Qwen development data, dropping only 1, 2 or 3 bits gives oracle rates of
59.8\%, 37.1\% and 19.5\%, so even a mild one-bit mantissa truncation is not
certifiable this way. By Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-ceiling}, no error propagation
algorithm with the same center and box representation can do better.

\paragraph{v21: exact search over the LM head does not prune.} The tree visits
99.7\% (Qwen) and 100\% (TinyLlama) of rows, and its metadata pushes $\rho$ above
one. Dimension-adaptive pruning removes rows only after most coordinates are read:
in a calibrated order it saves 17\% on Qwen and nothing on TinyLlama; rotating to
the SVD basis is no better. In dimensions 896--2{,}048 these bounds are too loose
relative to the gap between the top logit and the bulk of the vocabulary. This
rejects the designs we tested, not every exact search algorithm.

\paragraph{v22--v23: the head certificate is sound but refines too often.} In v22
the head is a BF16 matrix product on MPS whose BF16 output is then widened; in v23
it is an FP32 matrix product on MPS whose operands are the BF16 weights and hidden
state converted exactly to FP32. Across $1{,}024\,V$ logits per model and variant, no
computed logit left its enclosure, and no returned token differed from the exact
reference. With BF16 output, 7\% of positions need refinement; in an exploratory
ablation on development data, replacing the norm envelope $A_i$ by the exact
$\sum_k|w_{ik}h_k|$ changes this only from 10.2\% to 9.4\%, so the BF16 output
rounding term dominates. With FP32 output the rate falls to 0.68\% and 1.76\%, with
1.01--1.02 candidates per position on average. For Qwen the point estimate meets
the 1\% criterion but the interval reaches 1.17\%, so we do not claim a population
rate below 1\%. A refinement rate is not a latency: an unfused development
prototype added 44\% latency to the BF16 head, and no fused kernel was built.

\section{Limitations}
Two small models, one hardware platform, and one quantization family (mantissa
truncation or rounding of all Transformer projections) were tested. The oracle
ceiling covers coordinate-wise symmetric boxes with the coarse hidden state as
center; certificates that exploit error correlations, other centers, or other
quantizers are not excluded. The search experiments test specific bounds; v21c is
an SVD ablation, not a full FEXIPRO implementation. Byte ratios are logical, not
measured memory traffic. The head certificate is conditionally sound under the
stated arithmetic model; conformance of the MPS kernel to that model is supported
only by zero observed violations, not proven. No latency claim is made for v23. The preregistrations were not deposited in a
public registry before the experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
Across two pretrained language models, the evaluated symmetric-box certificates and
exact-search screening methods fail to meet their preregistered feasibility
thresholds. The oracle ceiling identifies a representation-level limitation that no
error-propagation algorithm with the same center can overcome, and its rigorous
verification leaves no unresolved positions. FP32 output substantially reduces the
refinement frequency of fixed-hidden-state greedy-head certification, although it
does not satisfy the preregistered criterion on both models. Practical latency
improvements and end-to-end numerical reproducibility remain unestablished. We
release the preregistrations and code so that others can run the oracle test on
their own quantizer before investing in a full certification pipeline.
